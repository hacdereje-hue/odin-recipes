Authentic Tacos al Pastor

Originating in Puebla, Mexico, this beloved street taco is a vibrant cross-cultural masterpiece born in the early 20th century when Lebanese immigrants introduced vertical spit-roasting (shawarma) to central Mexico. Local cooks adapted the technique using pork marinated in a rich, earthy blend of achiote paste, dried guajillo chiles, vinegar, and warm spices, slowly roasting the stacked meat on a spinning trompo topped with a whole fresh pineapple. As the meat turns, the pineapple juices drip down to tenderize and lightly sweeten the pork while the edges char into crispy, smoky perfection.

Ingredients

  • Pork shoulder
  • Achiote paste
  • Guajillo chiles
  • Pineapple
  • Corn tortillas
  • Onion
  • Cilantro

Steps

  1. Blend rehydrated guajillo chiles with achiote paste and spices, then marinate the sliced pork shoulder for at least 4 hours.
  2. Stack the marinated pork slices onto a vertical spit (trompo) with fresh pineapple on top.
  3. Roast slowly over high heat and shave off the crispy, caramelized outer layers of pork.
  4. Warm the corn tortillas on a hot griddle until soft and pliable.
  5. Fill the tortillas with shaved pork and top with diced onion, chopped cilantro, and roasted pineapple chunks.
