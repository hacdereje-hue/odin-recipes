Pasta Carbonara


What is Pasta Carbonara?

Carbonara is a quintessential Roman pasta classic with a rich history and an iconic flavor profile.
While some trace its origins to Italian charcoal workers (carbonari) cooking over open fires, others
attribute its name to the generous flecks of black pepper that resemble coal, or even a World War II
fusion of local pasta with American soldier rations of bacon and eggs.

What truly sets this dish apart is its signature technique: raw eggs, hard cheese, cured pork, and
black pepper are gently warmed by the heat of the hot pasta to create a silky, indulgent sauce.
Note that while the eggs are cooked by the pasta's heat, you can easily substitute pasteurized eggs if preferred.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon extra virgin olive oil or unsalted butter
  • 1/2 pound pancetta or thick cut bacon, diced
  • 3 to 4 whole eggs
  • 1 cup grated Parmesan or pecorino cheese
  • 1 pound spaghetti
  • Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste

Steps

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a rolling boil using 1 tablespoon of salt for every 2 quarts of water.
  2. Substitute pasteurized eggs if preferred.
  3. Cook the diced pancetta or bacon in a skillet with olive oil or butter until crispy, then remove from heat.
  4. Boil the spaghetti until al dente and reserve 1 cup of starchy pasta water before draining.
  5. Whisk the eggs, grated cheese, and black pepper together in a separate bowl.
  6. Toss the hot pasta with the pork off the heat and stir in the egg mixture with splashes of pasta water until creamy.
